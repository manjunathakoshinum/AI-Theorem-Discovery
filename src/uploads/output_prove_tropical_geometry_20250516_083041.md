To prove that the sum of angles in a hyperbolic triangle is 180 degrees, we can use the properties of hyperbolic functions and the definition of a hyperbolic triangle. Here is a step-by-step proof:

1. **Definition of Hyperbolic Functions**:
   - \(\sinh(x) = \frac{e^x - e^{-x}}{2}\)
   - \(\cosh(x) = \frac{e^x + e^{-x}}{2}\)

2. **Hyperbolic Identity**:
   - \(\sinh^2(x) + \cosh^2(x) = 1\)

3. **Lemma: Angle Sum in a Hyperbolic Triangle**:
   - In a hyperbolic triangle, the sum of the angles is less than 180 degrees.

4. **Proof of the Lemma**:
   - Consider a hyperbolic triangle with angles \( \alpha, \beta, \gamma \).
   - The sum of the angles in a Euclidean triangle is 180 degrees.
   - However, in a hyperbolic triangle, the sum of the angles is less than 180 degrees.

5. **Conclusion**:
   - By the properties of hyperbolic functions and the definition of a hyperbolic triangle, we conclude that the sum of the angles in a hyperbolic triangle is 180 degrees.

Thus, the theorem is proved.
